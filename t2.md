# T2 — Team Track Level 2

**The second stage of collaborative contest training.** The problems now need more than one idea, and the ideas have to be joined. T2 is where students learn foresight: seeing where a solution is going before committing to it.

## Who it is for

* Students who have completed T1, or arrive with equivalent experience.
* Students comfortable with the basics of contest problem solving who want to work on harder problems with a partner.
* Students who can commit **5–7 hours a week**.

## Curriculum

<a href="./books.md"><img class="cover-inline" src="./img/lps6-cover.jpg" alt="Learning Problem Solving VI"></a>

T2 follows the club's textbook **[Learning Problem Solving VI — From Foundations to Insight](./books.md)** — the same 54 chapters and 217 themes as T1, on a **second pass at a higher difficulty**. The book was written to be used this way: the easy problems the first year, the medium and hard ones the year after, so that a familiar chapter keeps yielding new ground.

What T2 emphasises:

* Anticipating several possible approaches before starting, and choosing between them.
* Sustaining a longer argument without losing the thread.
* Moving between algebra, number theory, combinatorics, and geometry inside a single problem.
* Proof writing: structure, completeness, and saying exactly what you mean.

Assignments and tests stop handing out step-by-step hints. Teams design their own strategy.

## The two-week cycle

**Week 1** — Study the chapter: theory, then the worked examples. Work the first half of the team assignment and submit it by Sunday. From the second cycle onward: official solutions to the previous assignment on Monday, grading and feedback from the previous test Monday–Thursday, selected teams preparing Friday to present on Sunday.

**Week 2** — Work the second half of the assignment and submit by Sunday. Official solutions Monday; review them as a team. **Sunday: the team test.**

## Tests

Each chapter has its own test: three problems on the chapter just studied, three from the other areas.

| Band | Problems | Points each | Questions per problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E — Easy** | 2 | 10 | 1 |
| **M — Medium** | 2 | 15 | 2 |
| **H — Hard** | 2 | 25 | 3 |

**Total 100 points. The pass mark is 51. Duration: 120 minutes.**

T1 and T2 study the same chapters, but their papers are almost entirely different — at most a single problem is shared. A T2 paper assumes the ideas of T1 are already available and asks what you can build with them.

Every problem is drawn from an international competition and rewritten for clarity. Solutions are published step by step, so parents can grade and can work through a paper afterwards with their child.

Teams choose their testing pace — weekly, every two weeks, or one test per chapter. See [Programs](./programs.md).

## Submitting and grading

One PDF per submission, through the form. No email, no photographs, no multiple files; submissions in the wrong format lose points.

All work is graded with comments. Selected teams present live on Sunday, or send a recording. Results and individual evaluations are published on the site.

## What you should be able to do by the end

* Recognise patterns and connections across different topics.
* Sustain a long, multi-step solution with a clear structure.
* Write a complete and logically sound proof.
* Work with a partner under time pressure.
* Explain a solution to other people with confidence.

Students who finish T2 well are ready for **[T3](./t3.md)**, where the problems become olympiad problems.
