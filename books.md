# The MCC Curriculum

The club's curriculum is **Learning Problem Solving VI and VII** — two volumes covering four years of training. Around them sit an entry book for students who are just starting out, a book of stories and puzzles, the tests and the yearbook, and a free online course.

*Mỗi hành trình toán học đều bắt đầu từ một tia sáng tò mò. Để giúp học sinh khởi đầu đúng hướng, chúng tôi đã thiết kế một lộ trình phát triển rõ ràng.*

### How the books are built

* Every chapter opens with **a puzzle worth arguing about at dinner**, then its themed sections.
* Roughly **30% of each volume is exercise problems**, spread evenly across difficulty and across Algebra, Combinatorics, Geometry, and Number Theory. The rest are worked examples, every one with a complete solution.
* There are **no one-step, plug-in-the-formula problems**. Easy or hard, every problem asks the student to read, analyse, compute, and check. Routine drills belong to other curricula; these books assume you want to think.
* Each section teaches **one idea deeply**. You may notice several problems circling the same piece of theory before the next section opens a different door — that is the design, not a lack of variety. A little material above grade level is deliberately mixed in, so that students meet the next idea before they need it.
* Problems without an attributed source are the author's own.
* **Use each book twice, over two years.** The first year (T1, or T3) works through the whole book, chapter 1 to the last, on the Easy and Medium problems; the second year (T2, or T4) starts again at chapter 1, on the Medium and Hard problems. This is why T1 and T2 share the same 54 chapters, and T3 and T4 the same 55.

### The four-year path

Together the two volumes are a complete four-year programme: **109 chapters, 433 themed sections, 2,721 problems, 109+ tests, and four Purple Comet campaigns.** A strong student can take a volume in a year; for most, two years per volume is the right pace.

<div class="note" markdown="1">
**This set is not for absolute beginners.** It assumes a student has already worked through Beast Academy or the AoPS Introductory series. Starting here without that footing is discouraging rather than useful.

## 2. Starting out — *100 Math Problems for Absolute Beginners*

**This is not the main course book.** It is for students who are just beginning, or who have been with the club a year or less — the book that comes *before* LPS VI, not instead of it.

Before diving into complex theory, students need to fall in love with patterns and logic. How can we make mathematics more enjoyable? Not with dry lessons, and not with a series of drills, but with something that sparks curiosity — something that feels like solving a mystery, discovering a pattern, unlocking a secret. Over the past ten years I have taught hundreds of students, from beginners to competition-level learners, and I have learned one thing: **the right problem, explained the right way, can change everything.**

I would recommend the print edition — it is a beautiful, high-quality book. Contact the club for a print copy. If you would rather have the e-book, it is in your hands in five minutes.

## 3. Stories and puzzles — *Four Roads* {#four-roads}

**The club's award book.** Mira, Bao, Tavi and Suri set out on four roads across Highmere. Past river landings, workshops and strange lands, every question opens a new road. Read straight through to follow the story, or stop with the characters and try each puzzle yourself. The puzzles use mathematics, logic and everyday science; hints and full solutions are in the book. For readers aged 10 and up.

**Volume One: 25 chapters, 75 puzzles.** The English and Vietnamese editions are checked line by line, so they have the same pictures, map, puzzles and answers. Brothers, sisters and friends with different editions can work on the same puzzles together.

Every chapter closes with its own test, and those tests are collected into the third book of the set — **226 papers and over 1,350 problems**, across all four levels. At the end of the year comes the yearbook, written together with the students.

## 5. Free online: The Online Mathematics Laboratory

**[TOML](./toml.html)** teaches mathematics investigated with a programming tool: explore a problem computationally, explain what you see, then establish it with a proof. Bilingual, with runnable Python on every page — copy it to your machine or run it in the browser. Free, with no book to buy.
