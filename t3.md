# T3 — Team Track Level 3

**Training for national olympiad problems.** The problems now demand real insight, standard olympiad machinery, and proof that would survive a grader. T3 is where problem solving turns into mathematical combat: an unfamiliar problem, a blank page, and a plan of attack.

## Who it is for

* Students who have completed T2 with strong results, or arrive with equivalent experience.
* Students comfortable with multi-step contest problems who want to push past AMC 10/12 toward national olympiads.
* Students who can commit **6–7 hours a week**.

## Curriculum

<a href="./books.md"><img class="cover-inline" src="./img/lps7-cover.jpg" alt="Learning Problem Solving VII"></a>

T3 follows the club's textbook **[Learning Problem Solving VII — From Insight to Mastery](./books.md)**.

The volume runs to **55 chapters** in five parts, 216 themed sections, and **1,326 problems** — **922 fully worked examples** and **404 exercises** — over 648 A4 pages, with most solutions rewritten from scratch. Every chapter opens with a puzzle. T3 is the **first pass** through it; T4 makes the second at mastery level.

What T3 emphasises:

* Olympiad-level work in algebra, number theory, combinatorics, and geometry.
* Applying the standard olympiad theorems and lemmas, and knowing when they apply.
* Structuring a rigorous, complete proof.
* Breaking an unfamiliar problem into parts that can actually be attacked.
* Reviewing and presenting team solutions so that everyone learns from them.

Assignments are longer and demand sustained effort. Several problems in a section may circle the same theory before the next section opens a different door — that is deliberate, and it is how depth is built.

## The two-week cycle

**Week 1** — Study the chapter: theory, then the worked examples. Work the first half of the team assignment and submit it by Sunday. From the second cycle onward: official solutions to the previous assignment on Monday, grading and feedback from the previous test Monday–Thursday, selected teams preparing Friday to present on Sunday.

**Week 2** — Work the second half of the assignment and submit by Sunday. Official solutions Monday; review them as a team. **Sunday: the team test.**

## Tests

Each chapter has its own test: three problems on the chapter just studied, three from the other areas.

| Band | Problems | Points each | Questions per problem |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E — Easy** | 2 | 10 | 1 |
| **M — Medium** | 2 | 15 | 2 |
| **H — Hard** | 2 | 25 | 3 |

**Total 100 points. The pass mark is 51. Duration: 150 minutes.**

"Easy" at this level means a mastery drill — a check that the fundamental tools are sharp — not a simple problem. The graduated questions inside each problem are there to reward a team that finds the right road even if it runs out of time before the end of it.

Every problem is drawn from an international competition and rewritten for clarity, with detailed step-by-step solutions published for grading.

Teams choose their testing pace — weekly, every two weeks, or one test per chapter. See [Programs](./programs.md).

## Submitting and grading

One PDF per submission, through the form. No email, no photographs, no multiple files; submissions in the wrong format lose points.

All work is graded with detailed comments. Selected teams present live on Sunday, or send a recording. Results and individual evaluations are published on the site.

## What you should be able to do by the end

* Apply advanced theorems and strategies across all four areas.
* Construct a rigorous, complete olympiad-style proof.
* Attack an unfamiliar problem by breaking it into solvable steps.
* Collaborate effectively over long stretches of time.
* Present an olympiad-level solution clearly.

Students who finish T3 well are ready for **[T4](./t4.md)**, and full international olympiad standards.
