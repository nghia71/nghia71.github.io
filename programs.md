## How a cycle works

Every track runs on a repeating **two-week cycle**.

| | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Study the chapter — theory and worked examples. Work the first half of the team assignment and submit by Sunday. Official solutions to the previous assignment are released on Monday; grading and feedback from the previous test are returned Monday–Thursday; selected teams prepare on Friday and present on Sunday. |
| **Week 2** | Work the second half of the assignment and submit by Sunday. Official solutions released Monday. Review them carefully as a team. **Sunday: team test.** One solution per problem, submitted jointly. |

Each chapter of the textbook opens four themed sections, and each chapter closes with its own test. Over a full year a team can cover the whole volume; most teams take two years per volume, which is the pace the books were designed for.

## Choose your own testing pace

Learning without testing does not stick. This year each family chooses how often their team sits a test, and can change that choice during the year:

* **Weekly** — the fastest pace. Exercises are not submitted; the test carries the load.
* **Every two weeks** — the standard pace, matching the cycle above.
* **One test per chapter** — for teams working through the material more slowly.

A student who knows they are weak in one area — geometry, say — can also ask for extra papers in that area and test on it repeatedly. Sitting more tests is how a student finds out where they actually stand.

## Teams

Students work in **teams of two**. Teams of three are possible with approval.

**Your team must already be formed when you register.** In past years we matched students afterwards, and it cost weeks — sometimes it did not work at all. A team only survives the year if the two students are at a similar level, can find a shared time to work and to sit tests, and actually want to work together. Time zones, swimming galas, and music recitals decide more teams than mathematics does. Please settle this before you register.

## Entrance and reassessment

Every student sits a placement test at the start of the year, unless they are exempt.

**New students** take the entrance test. It runs over **two days**; the two scores are averaged. If the first day goes much better or much worse than expected, the level can be adjusted before the second day — which is why papers are graded immediately after each sitting. A "fail" on day one is information, not a verdict.

**Returning students** are reassessed on the same schedule. Think of it as restarting the habits of last year rather than as a judgement. The exemptions, based on last year's results:

| Last year's result | Moving up a level | Staying, or moving down |
|---|---|---|
| **75% or above** | No test needed | No test needed |
| **Above 50%** | Must test | No test needed |
| **50% or below** | Must test | Must test |

The pass mark on any placement paper is **51 points out of 100**. Passing does not mean solving everything — the problems are deliberately broken into graduated questions so that a partial result still shows us where a student stands.

**If a student does not reach the bar**, the right next step is Beast Academy or the AoPS Introductory series, not a preparatory class. Two days of testing already show what a student can do; a few extra sessions do not change a wide gap, and starting a track that is out of reach helps nobody.

For the test format itself — how the six problems are built and scored — see [Tests & Yearbook](./tests.md).

## Awards through the year

🥇 **The MCC Championship** — the top 5 teams by average score across all tests during the year. The club's premier recognition of consistency and teamwork.

💯 **Top Perfect-Scorers** — the top 5 teams with the most perfect scores. This one rewards accuracy and persistence.

❄ **Snowflake Cup** *(December, just before Christmas)* — "every solution, like every snowflake, is unique." For the most elegant and best-written solutions of the first semester.

🥚 **The Mystical Egg** *(April, just before Easter)* — "every problem hides a clever trick — can you find it?" For ingenuity on puzzle-style problems.

📖 **Pi Challenge** *(monthly, September to June)* — every MCC student receives e-readership of *Pi Magazine*. Each month students receive the translated Pi problem-solving challenge in English; submissions go both to *Pi Magazine*, for international recognition, and to MCC teachers for our records. Winners are announced in June. This is the best training there is for writing to an international standard.

🌍 **Purple Comet! Math Meet** *(April)* — MCC teams compete internationally each spring. Top-performing teams are recognised publicly on [purplecomet.org](https://purplecomet.org).

🎁 **Special recognition** *(June)* — any individual or team achieving **three perfect scores** during the year receives a copy of *100 Problems for Absolute Beginners*, personally dedicated. Keep it or give it to a friend.

## Membership

The annual membership is **the same across all tracks**, and it is based on **last year's academic performance**. Students who achieved **more than 75% last year are exempt from paying**, whatever track they join this year. This keeps things fair and consistent.

Choosing a track is a personal decision that reflects each student's goals and how much they want to take on. This is a **club membership fee, not tuition for a particular track** — it reflects last year's results, not this year's choice. So two students in the same group may contribute different amounts, and that is not a matter of one being treated better than another.

If the contribution creates real difficulty for a family, please ask for a reduction or a waiver. And please remember that taking part in the club is entirely voluntary — if the structure does not suit your family, you are free not to continue.

## Submitting work

Assignments and tests are submitted through an online form:

* **One PDF file per submission.** Nothing else.
* **No email submissions.** No photographs, no image files, no multiple files.
* **Submissions in the wrong format lose points.** This is deliberate — it takes one term to become a habit, and then nobody has to be reminded again for the rest of the year.

All work is graded with comments. Results and per-student evaluations are published on the site rather than emailed, so students and parents can see not only a score but how it has changed over time.
