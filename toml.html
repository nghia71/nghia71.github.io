TOML

The Online Mathematics Laboratory — now the club's Coding Programme

First coding contest (Test 1): Sunday 4 October, 8:00–8:45 AM Victoria/BC time. Be at your computer, signed in on this page, by 7:45 AM. Everything a student needs to know is in Test 1, step by step below.

Who it's for

Every MCC student, Middle School (MS) and High School (HS) alike. If you're in the club, you can register for the coding programme.

How it works

  • Lessons, then contests. Before each contest, a lesson on the Lessons page shows, with fully worked examples, how problems like the contest's are solved — the math first, then a short Python program that expresses it. Lessons are open to everyone; no sign-in needed.
  • A contest every two weeks, alternating with a solution discussion the following fortnight, through the semester until just before Christmas.
  • Five problems per track. MS and HS each get their own paper: one problem each from Algebra, Combinatorics, Geometry and Number Theory, plus one harder problem, ordered Easy, Easy, Medium, Medium, Hard. Nobody is expected to finish — solve as many as you can.
  • After each contest, its problems are published as a new lesson with full solutions, and every submission gets written feedback, not just a score, once grading is done.

Test 1, step by step

Before Sunday

  1. Find your Student ID and access code in your registration confirmation. You need both. If you can't find them, ask your regional coordinator (Organization) before Sunday.
  2. Read Lesson 1 — ten worked examples, five per track, shaped exactly like the contest paper. Read its section on what a submission must look like closely: that is how your work is scored.
  3. Set up Python on the computer you'll use (a laptop or desktop, not a phone). Any Python 3 works: install it from python.org and use its IDLE editor, or any editor you already know. Check that you can write a small .py file, run it, and see what it prints. The Run buttons in Lesson 1 also work as a backup, right in the browser.
  4. Use the Google account whose email is on your registration. The contest papers are shared with that account at 7:55 AM, so be signed in to it in your browser (not a school or parent account). If your registration email is not a Google account, or you want to change it, tell your regional coordinator before Sunday.
  5. Sign in on this page once, on that same computer and browser — use the sign-in box below. This browser then remembers you, and on contest day the problem set and submission form appear here automatically.

Rules during the contest

  • Work on your own. No help from other people, and no AI tools, chatbots or online solvers.
  • Use only the Python libraries allowed in Lesson 1. Your program must run in 30 seconds or less, with no errors.
  • The math counts most: a program that only tries numbers until one fits, with no reasoning behind it, is not accepted even if it prints the right answer.

After the contest

The Test 1 problems and full solutions are published as Lesson 2 on the Lessons page once the contest is over. Scores and written feedback follow once grading is done.
