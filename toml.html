TOML

The Online Mathematics Laboratory — now the club's Coding Programme

First coding contest: 4 October, 8:00–8:45 AM Victoria/BC time. Arrive by 7:45 AM. Problems will look just like the worked examples in Lesson 1 — read through it before then.

Who it's for

Every MCC student, Middle School (MS) and High School (HS) alike. If you're in the club, you can register for the coding programme.

How it works

  • Lessons, then contests. A lesson (worked examples, like Lesson 1) goes out early in the week, showing how a problem like the upcoming contest's actually gets solved — the math ideas behind it, and how much they matter to the coding.
  • A contest every two weeks, alternating with a solution discussion the following fortnight — contest, then discussion, then repeat, through the semester until just before Christmas.
  • One sitting, MS and HS split. A session is generously sized — 15–20 problems or more — so nobody is expected to finish; the goal is doing as many as you're capable of, at your own level.
  • Feedback on every submission, not just a score, once grading is complete.

Lessons

Lesson 1 is live now — six Purple Comet 2004 problems worked in full (math, code, diagrams), with the complete rules for what a contest submission must look like. More problems from the same archive are added in batches over the coming weeks; each past contest also becomes its own lesson, with official solutions, once graded. Sign in with the student id and access code from your registration confirmation — the same sign-in works for every lesson as they're added.

The coding contest

A coding contest is one of the activities that rotate through the club's weekly Sunday session, on its own two-week cycle (contest, then solution discussion) — separate from the shared Sunday rotation described in Programs. See Organization for who runs it regionally.

Start / Submit form: link coming once the contest pipeline is live for the 4 October round. For that first round, expect a simpler, mostly-manual process — a shared read-only folder for the problem set and a plain submission form — while the fuller automated version (timed access, per-problem resubmission) is built and tested over the following contests.

What a submission must be

A single .py text file per problem — no PDFs, no Google Docs, no Jupyter notebooks. Full details, the allowed-library list, and the grading rubric are on Lesson 1 — read them before the contest, not during it.

Hall of Fame

Coming after the first contest is graded — a running total of problems solved, per student, across all contests.
